## Slide 1 — The opportunity: convert downtime signals into action

Farm operations can benefit when machine data, dealer service workflows, and operator signals are brought together before a disruption becomes extended downtime. The opportunity is to create a bounded telemetry workflow that detects actionable patterns, packages the evidence, and routes the next action to the right operating owner.

The target outcome is **cost optimization** through avoided unplanned repair and less disruptive field-service coordination. This is a qualitative value mechanism—not a verified financial outcome.

## Slide 2 — The market signal is real; the business case remains to be proven

John Deere positions Operations Center as a place to plan, monitor, analyze, and share farm data, while JDLink materials describe remote machine connectivity and diagnostics use cases.[1] [2]

USDA ERS identifies growing precision-agriculture adoption and differences by farm characteristics, supporting a focused segmentation question rather than a blanket market claim.[3]

The implication: connected-machine capabilities exist. The proof sprint must establish whether a specific farmer/dealer workflow will act on the signal often enough to create measurable value.

## Slide 3 — Customer and operating impact hypothesis

**Farm operator:** earlier warning, clearer action, less uncertainty during a time-sensitive field window.

**Dealer/service team:** a more useful service context before dispatch, allowing preparation rather than reactive triage.

**John Deere ecosystem:** a governed workflow connecting machine signals, service evidence, and outcome measurement.

The working hypothesis is intentionally narrow: reduce avoidable coordination friction around serviceable machine conditions. It must be tested through customer interviews, pilot telemetry, and service-log comparison.

## Slide 5 — Sponsor-owned value case defines the test boundary

The current sponsor-entered annual value range is **$500,000–$800,000**, with a midpoint of **$650,000**. The stated cost to prove is **$25,000** and the stated time to value is **18 months**.

Chart: sponsor input range with a midpoint marker and separate proof-cost marker. Label every number as *sponsor-entered assumption*.

The AI does not fabricate savings, revenue, or ROI. The proof sprint exists to replace assumptions with auditable evidence.

## Slide 6 — What the hackathon must prove

The proof sprint should produce a functional telemetry-to-action demonstration and an evidence package, not just a prototype.

1. Demonstrate a signal-to-service workflow using authorized, synthetic, or consented test data.
2. Show a traceable operator/dealer action path and the evidence needed to trigger it.
3. Capture limitations: connectivity, mixed-fleet interoperability, data quality, and human adoption.
4. Define pilot metrics: signal precision, time-to-triage, avoided coordination steps, and user acceptance.

## Slide 7 — Five specialist agents make proof evidence more complete

**UI/UX** assesses operator and service workflow clarity. **Cloud architecture** reviews integration and resilience. **Security** checks data boundaries. **Development quality** checks maintainability and test evidence. **Value/feasibility** checks the link between proof output and the sponsor’s hypothesis.

Each agent receives the same identity-redacted evidence packet, returns cited provisional findings, and cannot determine a winner or investment decision. Human judges retain final authority.

## Slide 8 — Decision path: evidence before escalation

The decision sequence is: opportunity evidence → sponsor economics → cited market validation → controlled proof sprint → specialist and Hackathon Agent evidence → human scorecard → indicator-backed realization.

At each gate, the next action is explicit: **hold**, refine the evidence, launch a limited pilot, or advance. The system does not turn a community vote, AI score, or leaderboard position into an investment decision.

## Slide 9 — Ask: authorize a bounded proof sprint

Authorize a 4–6 week proof sprint with an accountable sponsor, service/operations subject-matter expert, a representative user panel, and approved test data.

Success is not “winning the hackathon.” Success is a decision-ready evidence package: measurable workflow outcome, customer and dealer feedback, technical constraints, and a sponsor-owned recommendation to hold, refine, pilot, or advance.
