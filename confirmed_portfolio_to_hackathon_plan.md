# Confirmed Portfolio-to-Hackathon Operating Plan

## Governing Product Model

This product is an **enterprise idea-incubation portfolio**, not a conventional hackathon portal. Crowdsourcing is the front end of the innovation pipeline. A scheduled hackathon is a selective, time-bounded proof engine used only after executives choose the few business cases that merit deeper investment evidence.

The application must use **one primary operating record** for each selected idea. A user must not have to switch between a legacy Event HQ and a separate clean investment workspace to understand the work, evidence, or decision.

| Stage | Human owner | Required record and outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Idea incubation | Employee or contributor | An idea includes the problem, initial business case, potential value, duration, context, and supporting information. |
| 2. Crowdsourced enrichment | Peers and subject-matter contributors | Votes/endorsements, comments, evidence offers, ROI or value-range context, constraints, customer or operational observations, and related information remain attached to the idea. |
| 3. Executive portfolio selection | Sponsor and executives | Executives compare enriched ideas and select a small number of cases for a scheduled hackathon. Selection does not mean funding. |
| 4. Scheduled hackathon | Organizer, original idea owner, delivery team | Each selected business case becomes one hackathon project and inherits the crowdsource history, business case, value hypothesis, proof question, KPI/OKR links, and constraints. |
| 5. Traditional proof execution | Delivery team, mentors, original idea owner | Teams form, build, submit required evidence and demos, receive operational updates, and present the project. The idea owner remains connected as business/product owner. |
| 6. Advisory agent review | Governed Agent and Claude skills | Agents inspect the authorized BRD, technical documentation, code/repository, security, market evidence, demo, and business case. They create evidence gaps, preliminary quality findings, limitations, and judge-ready questions. They cannot choose winners or set investment gates. |
| 7. Human judging | Judges and executives | Judges review the original business objective, proof evidence, agent outcomes, questions raised during presentation, and independent rubric scores. Humans can agree, qualify, correct, or challenge agent findings. |
| 8. Executive continuation | Sponsor and executives | A human-entered heat map evaluates value, skill, and will. Executives decide whether to fund, advance assessment, return to proof, hold, or archive. Learning returns to the original business case. |

## Confirmed Decisions

| Decision | Confirmed behavior |
|---|---|
| Selected business case | Each executive-selected business case becomes a hackathon project inside the scheduled event. |
| Original contributor | The original idea submitter remains connected as business/product owner while delivery participants form or join the team. |
| Hackathon type | The event retains traditional hackathon behavior: team formation, mentor support, required submissions, demos, presentation questions, judges, scoring, and a ranked event outcome. |
| Agent role | The Hackathon Agent and Claude skills are non-binding preliminary reviewers that elevate human attention to gaps, quality, risk, and questions. |
| Human role | Humans own judging, corrections, rank, sponsor gate, continuation, and investment decisions. |
| Heat map | Humans enter a 1–5 score for six value dimensions plus two execution dimensions: skill and will. |

## Heat-Map Decision Model

The heat map is **not** an AI score, a popularity score, or a fabricated forecast. It is a transparent executive assessment recorded after the proof and human judging.

| Dimension | Human judgment question |
|---|---|
| Efficiency | Does the proof credibly improve speed, effort, throughput, or operational friction? |
| Productivity | Does the proof improve output or effectiveness for employees, dealers, customers, or operations? |
| Cost takeout | Does the proof demonstrate a credible path to avoiding or reducing cost, subject to sponsor validation? |
| Innovation | Does the proof show differentiated insight, approach, technology, or operating model? |
| Revenue growth | Does the proof create a credible revenue, retention, or commercial-growth opportunity, subject to sponsor validation? |
| Customer impact | Does the proof demonstrate meaningful customer, dealer, operator, or user benefit? |
| Skill | Does the organization and delivery team have the technical, operational, and business capability to execute? |
| Will | Is there clear ownership, sponsor commitment, adoption readiness, and willingness to execute? |

The result is a visible portfolio heat map across the selected projects. It helps executives distinguish, for example, a high-value idea with weak execution readiness from an achievable idea with limited business impact. It never replaces the recorded human rationale or sponsor gate.

## Single Primary Journey

The root experience will be reorganized around the following user-visible sequence:

1. **Innovation Portfolio**: open campaigns, incubated ideas, crowd enrichment, and executive shortlist.
2. **Executive Case Review**: one business case with original crowdsource context, sponsor value hypothesis, ROI/value context, KPI/OKR, and the selection decision.
3. **Scheduled Hackathon**: selected project portfolio, traditional event operations, team/idea-owner roles, required proof, communications, mentor support, and presentation readiness.
4. **Evidence and Advisory Review**: one proof record containing artifacts, Hackathon Agent results, eight Claude skill outputs, limitations, evidence gaps, and judge questions.
5. **Human Judging and Heat Map**: independent scorecards, agent corrections, ranked event outcome, and human-entered value/skill/will view.
6. **Executive Continuation**: sponsor gate, expected investment contribution, learning archive, and realization handoff returned to the original business case.

## Existing Capabilities to Preserve and Reposition

| Existing capability | Required treatment |
|---|---|
| Clean campaign, investment case, shared event, eight-skill Claude packet, human-only ranking, gate, and learning archive | Becomes the primary record and route structure. |
| Legacy Hackathon Agent, five specialist evaluators, repository audit, participant challenge, mentor routing, event communications, organizer copilot, reviewer calibration, and live event pulse | Be preserved as reusable capabilities inside the primary event record, not as a separate competing product journey. |
| Sponsor economics, research, voice/document intake, value-case cockpit, and realization data | Remain upstream and downstream evidence linked to the same selected business case. |

## Delivery Sequence and Acceptance Criteria

| Delivery step | Visible acceptance criterion |
|---|---|
| A. Portfolio reframing | The landing page plainly distinguishes crowd incubation from executive selection and scheduled hackathon proof. |
| B. Inherited project context | Opening a selected hackathon project visibly shows the originating idea, crowd context, business case, value hypothesis, KPI/OKR, and proof objective. |
| C. Traditional event operations | The shared event visibly supports participants, original idea owners, teams, mentors, communications, artifacts, demo/presentation readiness, and schedules. |
| D. Agent-as-preliminary-review | The project evidence record visibly combines Hackathon Agent and eight Claude skills as advisory evidence with citations, limitations, gaps, and judge questions. |
| E. Human decision heat map | Judges and executives can enter, inspect, and compare the six value dimensions plus skill and will without any automated score being substituted. |
| F. Continuation | The executive gate, expected contribution, learning, and realization handoff visibly return to the originating business case. |

## Explicit Non-Goals During Consolidation

The application will not add more independent legacy panels, introduce an AI-selected winner, treat votes as investment decisions, fabricate ROI or value scores, or create a new route that is not part of the portfolio-to-continuation sequence.
