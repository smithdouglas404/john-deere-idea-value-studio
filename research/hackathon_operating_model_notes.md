# Hackathon Operating-Model Research Notes

## Brightidea: Stages of a Hackathon

Source: https://www.brightidea.com/guide/hackathon/what-is-a-hackathon/stages/

- Treats a hackathon as an end-to-end program rather than an event-day submission contest: planning, pre-launch, event, and post-event closure.
- Planning needs sponsor goals, stakeholders, project plan, communications ownership, evaluation criteria, and a single source-of-truth event site.
- Challenge framing should align to enterprise priorities, participant expectations, and the eventual judging scorecard.
- Pre-launch work includes participant onboarding, sustained communications, eligibility/due diligence, and final-presentation expectations.

## Devpost for Teams: Organizer and Judging Operations

Source: https://info.devpost.com/blog/devpost-for-teams-features

- Frames the platform as a single place to manage internal hackathon information and operations.
- Core judging capabilities include judge invitations, custom criteria, scoring and commenting, judging groups, explicit judge-to-group assignment, multiple judging rounds, and organizer-only leaderboard monitoring.
- The central benchmark is not an AI evaluator; it is operational coherence around the event lifecycle and fair human review.

## Implication for the John Deere Studio audit

The primary experience must make one operating record visibly connect: sponsor challenge and investment thesis, event configuration, participant/team work, evidence, human review, decision, and post-event follow-through. AI skills should strengthen those handoffs rather than appear as a parallel workspace.

## Major League Hacking: Judging Plan

Source: https://guide.mlh.com/general-information/judging-and-submissions/judging-plan

- Treats judging design as an up-front operating concern rather than day-of logistics.
- Recommends a clear submission deadline, participant-visible judging schedule updates, planned judge allocation, and structured deliberation.
- Emphasizes that every team should reach an initial judging round after submissions are due and that judges need a practical process for questions and comparisons.

## Peer-reviewed corporate-hackathon continuation study

Source: Ratsoga, Z. & Primus, M. (2023), “Factors influencing post-hackathon project continuation in an African corporate setting,” *African Journal of Information and Communication*, 31. DOI: https://doi.org/10.23962/ajic.i31.15564

- Defines hackathons as time-limited, problem-oriented events in which teams form and build prototypes, but distinguishes event success from sustained post-event value.
- Reports three core continuation factors in the corporate case study: financing, team skills fit/diversity, and integration into company operations.
- The review reports research that structured post-hackathon activities, lessons learned, idea assessment, and budget decisions support better continuation than a winner-only event model.

## Implication for the John Deere Studio audit

The Studio’s investment gate and learning archive are directionally appropriate, but they must be part of the same visible event journey as team formation, evidence submission, human review, and final decision. Otherwise the product still behaves like a conventional event platform plus a separate investment tool.
