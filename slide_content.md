### Slide 3 — The problem to solve
**Title:** The build is no longer the bottleneck. Decision-quality is.

**Message:** Generative tools can accelerate challenge design, ideation, prototyping, and pitch preparation. The risk is that a faster event produces more prototypes—without a faster way to identify value, test assumptions, or place a credible idea into an accountable roadmap.

| Traditional hackathon outcome | The unresolved value question |
| --- | --- |
| Energy, visibility, and a working prototype | Which customer or operational problem is important enough to solve? |
| A selected “winner” | What value range is credible, and what assumptions drive it? |
| A point-in-time demo | What proof is missing before a sponsor should commit resources? |
| A repository or follow-up deck | Who owns the next decision and when is it made? |

**Visual direction:** Split-screen contrast. Left: a tight “48-hour event” timeline collapsing into untracked artifacts. Right: a calm evidence trail leading to an investment gate. Highlight the lost handoff in muted yellow.

### Slide 4 — The proposal
**Title:** Introduce the Value Fieldbook.

**Message:** The Value Fieldbook is a continuous, AI-assisted operating model that retains the inclusion and energy of crowdsourcing, but replaces event-day scoring with a disciplined progression from field signal to investable value case.

**Core design:**

1. **Crowd-sourced signals** gather grounded observations from employees, dealers, customers, and partners.
2. **AI-assisted shaping** synthesizes recurring themes, clarifies the problem, identifies stakeholders, and drafts an evidence-linked value hypothesis.
3. **Focused proof sprints** test the one assumption most likely to change the investment decision.
4. **Recurring investment gates** decide whether to advance, reshape, pause, or stop the case.

**Visual direction:** A field-to-fund journey with four contour-line stages: Capture → Shape → Prove → Decide. Use the seed-to-signal mark as the starting point and an upward harvest-yellow path as the progression line.

### Slide 5 — What changes, and what stays
**Title:** Preserve participation. Change the unit of progress.

| Keep from the hackathon | Replace with the Value Fieldbook |
| --- | --- |
| Broad access to ideas and diverse voices | Always-on signal capture across the customer and operating ecosystem |
| Rapid collaboration and prototype energy | Short, evidence-focused micro-sprints aimed at the highest-risk assumption |
| Friendly competition and visibility | Transparent progression based on value, evidence, delivery fit, and named ownership |
| Final judging event | Recurring decision gates with an explicit next action, sponsor, and date |
| Showcase of working demos | Portfolio of value cases with an auditable route to funding or closure |

**Callout:** The output is not “the best hack.” The output is a portfolio of decisions that leaders can govern.

**Visual direction:** Light editorial comparison table. Use contour bands, not generic checkmarks. Make the final callout a deep-green strip with harvest-yellow type.

### Slide 6 — How AI earns its place
**Title:** AI accelerates the value work; people retain the judgment.

| AI co-pilot role | Human accountability |
| --- | --- |
| Cluster duplicate or related signals | Decide which opportunity merits attention |
| Draft challenge briefs, research prompts, and value hypotheses | Validate customer context and operational realities |
| Reveal assumptions, evidence gaps, and comparable patterns | Set the value range and risk tolerance |
| Summarize experiment results and gate materials | Sponsor, fund, reshape, or stop the work |

**Message:** Every AI output should expose its sources, logic, confidence, and missing evidence. The model becomes more trustworthy by making uncertainty visible.

**Visual direction:** A transparent “AI rationale” card emerging from a field-research notebook. Use the generated evidence still life (`/manus-storage/jd-value-studio-evidence_9dc0e750.jpg`) as a low-opacity background element.

### Slide 8 — Illustrative value logic
**Title:** Make the value case early—and make it honest.

**Message:** An initial value case is a range, not a promise. It gives the team a shared economic hypothesis and makes the proof plan explicit.

| Value lens | Example measures | Evidence to seek |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer outcomes | Uptime, operator time, input savings, adoption, retention | Customer interviews, telemetry, service records, usage data |
| Operating outcomes | Rework, parts availability, service-cycle time, quality, safety | Process data, frontline observation, baseline performance |
| Strategic outcomes | Differentiation, subscription attach, ecosystem leverage, sustainability | Market signal, partner input, portfolio fit, regulatory context |
| Delivery reality | Data readiness, integration complexity, operating ownership, risk | Architecture review, delivery capacity, policy and control checks |

**Callout:** The first objective is not precision. It is to discover quickly whether the decision would change if the assumption proved false.

**Visual direction:** Four vertical field strips with soil, plant, machinery, and horizon textures. Use a yellow “assumption under test” annotation tag across the center.

### Slide 9 — A focused six-week activation
**Title:** Start small, learn fast, leave with an operating cadence.

| Week | Focus | Tangible outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Define one priority domain and decision criteria | Sponsor alignment, participant map, value taxonomy, guardrails |
| 2 | Capture and synthesize signals | Curated opportunity field with traceable sources and theme clusters |
| 3 | Shape the top candidates | Draft problem statements, stakeholder maps, value ranges, evidence gaps |
| 4 | Run proof micro-sprints | Experiment plans and targeted demonstrations focused on decision-changing assumptions |
| 5 | Build the investment field | Comparable value cases, confidence levels, delivery considerations, named sponsors |
| 6 | Facilitate the first gate | Decisions to advance, reshape, pause, or close; repeatable cadence and backlog |

**Message:** This is an activation design, not a delivery commitment. Scope, participant groups, governance controls, and commercial assumptions should be confirmed with John Deere.

**Visual direction:** Horizontal topographic timeline. Use subtle terrain elevation at each week, with milestone pins in harvest yellow.

### Slide 10 — What success looks like
**Title:** Measure the pipeline, not just the participation.

**Leading indicators:** signal diversity; signal-to-theme synthesis time; time to an initial value case; percentage of cases with a named sponsor; evidence-gap closure rate.

**Decision indicators:** percentage of cases advanced, reshaped, paused, or stopped with documented rationale; elapsed time from signal to gate; share of portfolio value tied to validated evidence.

**Outcome indicators:** realized customer or operating benefits from advanced cases; rework avoided; adoption and renewal support; portfolio capacity reallocated away from low-confidence initiatives.

**Closing line:** *A better idea program is not a louder innovation event. It is a clearer path from experience to value to decision.*

**Visual direction:** Use the generated abstract agricultural progression landscape (`/manus-storage/jd-value-studio-landscape_dbd51e8c.jpg`) with a light gradient overlay. Place the three indicator groups as an ascent along the harvest-yellow path.

### Slide 11 — Discussion and next decision
**Title:** Choose the first field to cultivate.

**Discussion prompts:**

1. Which customer or operating domain has high-value signals today but no consistent route to an investment decision?
2. Which senior sponsor can own the first recurring gate?
3. Which existing data sources, customer forums, dealer channels, and employee groups should form the first signal field?
4. What evidence would materially change the decision for the first two or three candidates?

**Closing statement:** We recommend co-designing a bounded first activation around one domain, a transparent value taxonomy, and a visible decision cadence—then using the output to shape the long-term innovation operating model.
