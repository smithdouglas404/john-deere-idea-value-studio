# Verified competitor research — implementation reference

## Brightidea

Brightidea presents a broad innovation-management suite covering idea capture, crowdsourcing programs, hackathons, experiment and lab tracking, and wider innovation ecosystem work. Its public materials describe stage-gate workflows, idea capture and tracking, hackathon coordination, and progress tracking. The Value Fieldbook opportunity is not basic event coordination; it is a single governed proof record that binds source evidence, sponsor assumptions, authorized code and documents, AI advisory, participant improvement work, and a human investment decision.

Source: https://www.brightidea.com/product/

## Devpost

Devpost’s public judging guidance describes established organizer-led judging panels and community mentorship around hackathon participation. The application must therefore retain explicit panel assignment, cited evidence inspection, participant challenges, reviewer rationale, recusal, and human-owned final decisions rather than treating AI output as a judge.

Source: https://help.devpost.com/article/191-judging-at-devpost-hackathons

## TAIKAI

TAIKAI publicly advertises virtual hackathon organization, project submission, hackathon discovery, and a leaderboard. These are baseline event mechanics. The Value Fieldbook differentiator must be turning a submission into a traceable proof record that can change a real investment decision.

Source: https://taikai.network/

## HackerRank Engage

HackerRank Engage publicly emphasizes AI-assisted event construction, branded event sites, coding challenges, promotion, and developer engagement. The Value Fieldbook must exceed event creation with AI evidence intelligence: source provenance, cross-skill disagreement, market challenge, value-case stress testing, and separately recorded human judgment.

Source: https://www.hackerrank.com/products/engage

## Operating conclusion

Conventional platforms establish registration, teams, submission, communication, challenge hosting, and human judging as baseline. The planned net-new Value Fieldbook capabilities must be embedded in the lifecycle, not surfaced as additional destinations: (1) evidence graph and claim traceability, (2) multi-modal proof review, (3) cross-skill deliberation, (4) market challenge, (5) value-case stress test, (6) team improvement coaching, and (7) human review copilot.

## Modern hackathon operating baseline

HackerEarth’s organizing guide treats a hackathon as a time-bound, purpose-led proof event: the organizer defines a business problem and focused theme, provides relevant context, forms cross-functional teams, supports participants with mentors, evaluates novelty, feasibility, simplicity, design, and business value, and establishes a deployment or downstream route. The guide also describes two-phase formats that separate broad ideation from prototype work and later selection.

Source: https://www.hackerearth.com/community-hackathons/resources/e-books/guide-to-organize-hackathon/

Devpost for Teams describes an enterprise baseline of event landing information, registration, schedule, team-building, submission requirements, judging criteria, judge groups and assignments, multiple judging rounds, announcements, project cataloging, and reporting. Its public guidance makes the organizer responsible for rules, judging, winners, prizes, and communication.

Sources: https://info.devpost.com/blog/devpost-for-teams-features and https://help.devpost.com/article/307-how-hackathons-on-devpost-work

TAIKAI emphasizes end-to-end event design, participant/mentor/judge coordination, custom participant materials, workshops, live support, and post-event follow-through.

Source: https://taikai.network/en/organize-your-hackathon

## Implication for Value Fieldbook

The platform must simplify around the hackathon operating sequence—not panel accumulation: purpose and value case, event and proof design, team and mentor mobilization, evidence-bearing submission, assigned human review, decision ownership, then realization. AI belongs inside those steps as traceable evidence and improvement support, never as a replacement event or decision process.
